Your measurements

Use a soft tape and tie elastic around your natural waist.

Before you measure

Wear the bra or undergarments you plan to wear with the finished garment. Stand naturally and use a mirror or ask someone to help.

Your natural waist is where your torso creases when you bend to the side. Keep the elastic there for all waist measurements.

Use three small marks on each side of your torso, where a shirt’s side seam would sit. Put bits of low-tack tape on a thin, close-fitting layer, or use a washable skin-safe marker on bare skin.

  1. Underarm. Mark about one finger below the middle of the armpit crease, on your torso.
  2. Bust level. Below that point, mark where the level tape crosses your side when it wraps around the fullest part of your bust.
  3. Waist. Mark where that same imaginary side seam meets your waist elastic.

Use the same left and right dots for the front and back arcs at each level. Keep the tape snug without pulling.

From paper to a fitted block

A sloper is a close-fitting template you can adapt into sewing patterns.

Print at 100%

Choose US Letter, single-sided, and Actual Size. Print the guide page first. Its test square should measure exactly 2 × 2 in (50.8 × 50.8 mm).

Trim, match, and tape

Follow the page map and cut out the gray frames. Butt matching codes together and tape from the back. Trace the front and back separately.

Sew a test bodice

Fold the paper darts and smooth the seams across them. Add seam allowance before cutting stable woven fabric such as muslin.

Cutting, sewing, and fitting your test bodice
  1. Trace your pieces. Transfer the darts, bust line, grainline, and center lines. Cut one front on the fold and two mirrored backs for a center-back opening. Add seam allowance where you’ll sew or pin, including center back, shoulders, and side seams. Keep the center front on the fold.
  2. Sew and try it on. Baste the darts from the wide ends toward their points, then baste shoulders and side seams. Pin the center back along the stitching line, using a helper if possible. Try it on over the same undergarments you measured in.
  3. Check the fit. The waist line should sit at your waist, the shoulder seam should end at the shoulder joint, and the side seams should hang straight. Check comfort as you breathe and move your arms. If fabric pulls or gapes, mark the adjustment on the test bodice and transfer it back to your paper block.
  4. Keep the final block. Trace the adjusted stitching lines, check adjoining seams meet smoothly, and mark the date. Use it to develop patterns for woven garments.
